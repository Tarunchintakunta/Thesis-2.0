\begin{abstract}
Building energy models are trained on sensor streams that, in operation, are often incomplete: sensors fail, controllers drop off the network and some points are never installed. This project tests whether a physics-informed machine-learning model predicts hourly HVAC energy more reliably than conventional models when input sensors are missing. The study uses the public three-year dataset of LBNL Building 59, an office building in Berkeley, California, and adopts the target, test window (July--December 2020) and metrics of the principal baseline, the Bayesian neural network of \citet{mahajan2024bnn}, which assumes complete, forward-filled data.

The proposed PI-GRU is a mask-aware recurrent network that jointly predicts HVAC energy and indoor temperature and is regularised by a first-order resistance--capacitance heat balance with learnable, positive coefficients. It is compared with a re-implementation of the baseline BNN, a sensor-free profile, XGBoost, LSTM and GRU models and two ablations, under identical random (MCAR) and contiguous multi-sensor outages that remove 10--40\% of the readings, with five seeds, day-block bootstrap intervals and Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon tests.

On complete data the PI-GRU beats the published baseline on every metric: RMSE 9.01 against 9.65\,kWh, MAE 5.70 against 7.06\,kWh and MAPE 0.22 against 0.35. Under 40\% contiguous outages it is 20\% more accurate than the re-implemented baseline. XGBoost on the same mask-aware features has the lowest RMSE (7.97\,kWh), and the physics term is neutral for accuracy relative to an architecture-matched twin, while lowering the heat-balance residual by 10\% on complete data. Random sensor loss barely affects any model, contiguous outages raise errors by 15--23\%, and observation-mask features are the most effective single protection. A least-squares fit shows that the envelope time constant is not identifiable from this tightly controlled building, which explains the weak contribution of the physics prior. The code, data and a notebook that runs on Colab, Jupyter, macOS and Windows are released.

\medskip
\textbf{Keywords:} physics-informed machine learning, HVAC energy prediction, missing sensor data, smart buildings, robustness, LBNL Building 59
\end{abstract}
