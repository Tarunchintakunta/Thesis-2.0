Student: Anjaneya Reddy Gurram ID: 24288853 Date: September 2026
Thesis: Reliability and Recovery of Amazon SQS Messaging under Injected Consumer and Downstream Failures

Research Question

How does Amazon SQS configuration affect message reliability and recovery under injected consumer and downstream failures?

This research introduces the Dynamic Intelligent Visibility Extender (DIVE) algorithm, which dynamically adjusts SQS visibility timeout per-message based on retry count, bridging the gap between steady-state queue optimisation and failure-regime behaviour. The three articles below are the most directly related works from the literature review: the baseline being extended, the chaos-engineering methodology being adapted, and the architectural resilience comparison that frames the research niche.

Article 1 — Baseline Paper

Article 1

Optimization of SQS Configurations for Efficient Batch Data Processing

Kyrychenko, O.O., Ostapov, S.E. and Kyrychenko, O.L.

WSEAS Transactions on Systems, 24, pp. 36–43 (2025)

DOI: 10.37394/23202.2025.24.4

SQS Configuration Queueing Theory Batch Processing Serverless

Summary

Kyrychenko et al. investigate how to optimally configure Amazon SQS for high-volume distributed data processing. Using classical queueing theory (M/M/1 and M/M/k models), the authors evaluate response time, queue length, and utilisation rate across different batch-size and visibility-timeout combinations, testing against 500,000 records in a Python/Boto3-based pipeline. Their central finding is that batch size and message visibility time are the dominant factors affecting system performance, and that well-tuned configurations can markedly boost throughput while preserving reliability.

Relevance to Thesis

This paper is the direct baseline that the thesis replicates and then extends. Kyrychenko et al. optimise visibility timeout (10–900s), delivery delay, and batch size purely for throughput under steady-state conditions where consumers do not fail. The thesis uses their exact parameter ranges and throughput/latency metrics as the control arm, then subjects those same configurations to injected consumer and downstream failures — the regime in which visibility timeout, retry limit, and DLQ threshold actually become consequential. The DIVE algorithm exists precisely to address the limitation exposed when Kyrychenko et al.'s "optimal" settings are placed under failure conditions.

Gap exposed: Reliability is asserted as a service guarantee rather than measured. The paper's M/M/1 formalism has no state for a failed consumer, meaning the parameters it tunes (visibility timeout, retry limit) are behaviourally inert in the model — they activate only when processing fails, which is the regime the model excludes by construction.

Article 2 — Methodology Precedent

Article 2

Exploring the Impact of Chaos Engineering with Various User Loads on Cloud Native Applications: An Exploratory Empirical Study

Al-Said Ahmad, A., Al-Qora'n, L.F. and Zayed, A.

Computing, 106(7), pp. 2389–2425 (2024)

DOI: 10.1007/s00607-024-01292-z

Chaos Engineering Fault Injection Cloud-Native Serverless & Containers

Summary

Al-Said Ahmad et al. empirically investigate cloud-native application reliability by injecting delay-based faults into both serverless (Lambda) and containerised microservice workloads under varying user loads. A key finding is that performance degradation is load-dependent rather than uniform: at a particular injected delay threshold, "defects and availability problems appear out of nowhere." The study demonstrates that chaos engineering can uncover failure modes invisible under normal testing, validating fault injection as a rigorous empirical method for cloud-native systems.

Relevance to Thesis

This paper provides the methodological precedent for the thesis's fault-injection approach. Al-Said Ahmad et al. establish that (a) injecting faults into serverless functions is a validated empirical technique, and (b) failure effects are non-linear and load-dependent — both findings that directly inform the thesis's experimental design. The thesis adapts this chaos-engineering methodology but redirects it: instead of measuring system-level throughput and latency under delay injection, it measures message-level outcomes (loss, duplication, DLQ capture, recovery time) under consumer-kill, unhandled-error, datastore-reject, and datastore-timeout failures.

Gap exposed: The study injects only delay-type faults and reports only system-level metrics (throughput, latency). It yields no message-level reliability data (loss, duplicates, dead-letter captures) and does not treat queue configuration as an experimental variable — precisely the space the thesis occupies.

Article 3 — Architectural Context

Article 3

Assessing the Impact of Asynchronous Communication on Resilience and Robustness: A Comparative Study of Microservice and Monolithic Architectures

Bosilia, N., Weinberger, G. and Haindl, P.

Software Architecture. ECSA 2025 Tracks and Workshops, Lecture Notes in Computer Science 15982. Cham: Springer, pp. 171–186 (2025)

DOI: 10.1007/978-3-032-04403-7_16

Event-Driven Architecture Resilience Async Communication Microservices

Summary

Bosilia et al. construct functionally equivalent monolithic and event-driven (microservice) implementations of an order-processing system, then compare their resilience and robustness under failure. Their central finding is that adopting asynchronous communication materially alters both resilience and robustness relative to synchronous designs — the event-driven architecture absorbs failures that the monolithic counterpart propagates. The study is published in the proceedings of the European Conference on Software Architecture (ECSA 2025), a CORE-B ranked venue.

Relevance to Thesis

This paper provides the architectural framing for the thesis. Bosilia et al. establish that queue-based (async) architectures are measurably more resilient than synchronous ones — but their comparison holds queue configuration fixed while varying the architecture around it. This means resilience is attributed to the architectural pattern rather than to any specific SQS setting. The thesis inverts this design: it holds the asynchronous architecture constant and varies the queue configuration (visibility timeout, retry limit, DLQ threshold) under injected failure. The synchronous arm in the thesis serves as the same kind of control that Bosilia et al. use, but the object of investigation is the configuration within the queue, not the queue's existence.

Gap exposed: Varies architectural style under failure but holds queue configuration constant. Resilience is attributed to the architecture, not to any setting within it. The thesis asks the complementary question: which configuration produces the resilience that Bosilia et al. observe?
