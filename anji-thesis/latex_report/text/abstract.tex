\begin{abstract}

Cloud-native applications increasingly rely on asynchronous message queues for decoupling, scalability, and fault isolation. Amazon Simple Queue Service (SQS) is a widely adopted managed queueing service that provides at-least-once delivery guarantees and integrates seamlessly with AWS Lambda. While prior research has optimized SQS configuration for steady-state performance~\citep{Kyrychenko2025}, the behavior of visibility timeout, \texttt{maxReceiveCount}, and batch size parameters under consumer and downstream failures remains unquantified.

This research investigates the research question: \textbf{How does Amazon SQS configuration affect message reliability and recovery under injected consumer and downstream failures?} A controlled experimental artefact was developed using AWS Serverless Application Model (SAM), implementing a two-arm comparison (synchronous API vs. SQS queue) with an in-process fault injection switch capable of triggering consumer kills, unhandled errors, datastore rejections, and datastore timeouts at deterministic times. A discrete-event simulator enables reproducible experiments without AWS account costs.

The study measures message loss, duplicate processing, dead-letter queue (DLQ) traffic, recovery time, throughput, and latency across local-simulator campaigns spanning visibility timeout (30–600\,s in confirmatory cells), \texttt{maxReceiveCount} (1–10), batch size (1–50), and four fault types. Three confirmatory tests from \texttt{stats\_H1\_H2\_H3.json} (\texttt{backend: localsim}) are reported with Holm--Bonferroni correction.

Key findings (evidence only; packaging-deduped $n=5$/cell, 350 design runs): (1) Queue-arm loss rate was identically 0.0 across VT levels in campaign~A, so the VT$\rightarrow$loss confirmatory test is not estimable (fail to reject; $p_{\mathrm{adj}}=1$). (2) Exploratory recovery time tracks visibility timeout nearly 1:1 under consumer kill. (3) MRC$\rightarrow$recovery (campaign~B) fails to reject ($H=2.43$, $p_{\mathrm{adj}}=1.0$); exploratory DLQ capture vs MRC is strong (MRC\,1 mean $\approx0.294$ vs $\approx0$ at MRC$\ge5$; $H=18.36$, $p=3.7\times10^{-4}$). (4) H3\_recovery fails to reject after Holm: Mann--Whitney $U=62.5$, raw $p=0.021$, $p_{\mathrm{adj}}=0.084$, rank-biserial $r=0.667$; throughput-favourable VT\,600/batch\,50 mean recovery $600$\,s vs alternatives mean $224$\,s (descriptive). No experimental cost DV was measured; cost figures elsewhere are estimator projections only. Adaptive visibility (DIVE) was not enabled in the committed matrix (\texttt{adaptive\_vt: false}).

The research contributes simulator-backed evidence on SQS resilience configuration, a reproducible fault injection framework, and quantitative guidance for practitioners. Results indicate that visibility timeout is the dominant factor in recovery time under the tested faults. Live AWS validation remains future work.

Future work includes live AWS validation to quantify simulator fidelity, extension to FIFO queues and multi-region architectures, and comparison with alternative brokers (Kafka, RabbitMQ). The complete artefact (SAM template, simulator, tests, committed run manifests, figures, statistical analysis) is provided in the repository for reproducibility and extension.

\end{abstract}
