\begin{abstract}

Serverless computing platforms employ at-least-once invocation semantics, delegating idempotency concerns to application developers. While custom serverless runtimes achieve exactly-once state mutation through asymmetric logging (e.g., Halfmoon by \citet{Qi2025Halfmoon}), managed platforms such as AWS Lambda expose only application-level primitives---conditional writes and idempotency-key patterns---to mitigate duplicate mutations during retries. Prior work has not empirically measured the duplicate-mutation reduction, latency overhead, and consumed capacity across these application-level strategies on unmodified managed infrastructure under a known retry schedule.

This research presents a controlled experimental evaluation of three write paths (P1 unconditional put, P2 conditional put, P3 idempotency-key pattern) for an AWS Lambda function writing to Amazon DynamoDB. The experiment injects known retry counts (1, 2, and 5 deliveries per request) by forcing timeouts after successful commits, then measures duplicate-mutation rates, end-to-end latency, and consumed DynamoDB capacity units. A pilot study (50 requests per path) determines replication size via the variance-driven rule from \citet{Wen2025Variance}; the full campaign collects data for approximately 1000 requests per path per multiplicity, analysed with 95\% confidence intervals and Holm–Bonferroni-corrected hypothesis tests.

Functional validation on the moto testing library confirms that P1 produces 100\% duplicate mutations at retry multiplicities 2 and 5, while P2 and P3 reduce the rate to 0\%, with P3 consuming approximately 1.5 additional capacity units per request relative to P2. Statistical testing (z-tests, chi-square, Mann–Whitney U) supports pre-registered expectations that guarded paths achieve greater than 90\% reduction in duplicate mutations at multiplicity 5, though wider confidence intervals at lower sample sizes leave the multiplicity-2 threshold inconclusive pending the live AWS campaign.

The findings position application-level conditional writes and idempotency-key persistence as effective duplicate-mitigation strategies on stock managed platforms, albeit with measurable capacity and latency costs. Unlike runtime-replacement approaches (Halfmoon, Boki, CausalMesh, LambdaStore), these primitives require no platform modification, trading the stronger exactly-once guarantees of custom runtimes for immediate applicability on AWS Lambda. Future work may extend the evaluation to multi-item transactions, provisioned capacity modes, and alternative managed serverless platforms to assess the generalisability of these trade-offs.

\end{abstract}
