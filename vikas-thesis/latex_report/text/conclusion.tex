\chapter{Conclusions and Discussion}

This chapter summarises the research contributions, discusses whether objectives were met, provides practitioner guidance, reflects on the methodology, and outlines future work.

\section{Research Contributions}

This study makes three contributions to the serverless computing and cloud systems literature:

\subsection{Empirical Measurement of Application-Level Idempotency Strategies}

Prior work on serverless fault tolerance has focused primarily on custom runtime solutions (Halfmoon, Boki, CausalMesh, LambdaStore, Styx, Netherite, $\mu$2sls) that achieve exactly-once or strong consistency guarantees by replacing or augmenting the FaaS platform \citep{Qi2025Halfmoon, Jia2024Boki, Zhang2024CausalMesh, Mast2026LambdaStore, Psarakis2025Styx, Burckhardt2022Netherite, Kallas2023Mu2sls}. While these systems demonstrate significant advances, they are unavailable to practitioners on managed platforms such as AWS Lambda, Google Cloud Functions, or Azure Functions.

This study implements a controlled measurement of the three application-level write strategies documented by AWS: plain unconditional writes (P1), conditional writes (P2), and idempotency-key patterns (P3). Moto confirms plumbing (0\% vs 100\% duplicates in-mock). A live pilot exists; the full campaign does not. Practitioner-facing quantitative cost claims await live campaign cells. P4/\texttt{TransactWriteItems} is out of scope.

\subsection{Pilot-First Replication Methodology for Serverless Experiments}

\citet{Wen2025Variance} demonstrate that serverless latency exhibits up to 338.76\% variance, yet only 38\% of top-venue papers report multiple runs or confidence intervals. This study adopts a pilot-first methodology: run a small pilot experiment (50 requests per path), estimate variance, and use power analysis to determine the full-campaign sample size for statistically significant results with tight 95\% confidence intervals. The pilot-sizing logic is implemented in \texttt{analysis/pilot\_size.py} and documented in \texttt{results/pilot\_report.md}, providing a reusable template for future serverless performance studies.

\subsection{Reproducible Artefact and Open Methodology}

The complete artefact---Lambda function, driver, analysis pipeline, infrastructure-as-code, and test suite---is provided in \texttt{vikas-thesis/lambda-idempotency-eval/}. The artefact is:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reproducible:} Deterministic seeding, pinned versions (Python 3.12, boto3 1.35.36, Terraform 1.15.7), and Makefile targets enable exact replication.
    \item \textbf{Tested:} 63 passing pytest tests cover all modules; \texttt{make functional} validates the pipeline on moto.
    \item \textbf{Documented:} Configuration manual (\texttt{docs/CONFIGURATION\_MANUAL.md}), analysis plan (\texttt{docs/ANALYSIS\_PLAN.md}), and ethics declaration (\texttt{docs/ETHICS.md}) provide step-by-step instructions.
    \item \textbf{Safe:} Bounded spend (USD 0.17 estimated), budget alarm (USD 1.00), and max-invocation limit (60,000) protect against runaway costs.
\end{itemize}

Researchers can replicate the experiment on their own AWS accounts, extend it to other platforms (Google Cloud, Azure), or adapt the methodology to study other managed services (e.g., Azure Cosmos DB conditional writes).

\section{Were the Objectives Met?}

The research question asked:

\begin{quote}
\textit{By how much do a conditional write (P2) and an idempotency-key write (P3) reduce duplicate state mutation, relative to a plain unconditional write (P1), when an AWS Lambda function is retried under injected timeouts---and what do they cost in end-to-end latency and consumed DynamoDB capacity?}
\end{quote}

\subsection{Objective 1: Quantify Duplicate-Mutation Rates}

\textbf{Status:} Partially met (moto plumbing + live pilot at multiplicity 2; full live campaign deliveries empty).

The moto functional validation quantifies duplicate-mutation rates for P1, P2, and P3 under known injected retry multiplicities of 1, 2, and 5:

\begin{itemize}
    \item P1: 100\% duplicate mutations at multiplicities 2 and 5 (all retries cause a second write).
    \item P2: 0\% duplicate mutations (conditional checks fail on retry, preventing the second mutation).
    \item P3: 0\% duplicate mutations (idempotency-key pattern skips the business write on retry).
\end{itemize}

Statistical tests (two-proportion $z$-test, chi-square) decisively reject the null hypothesis of equal duplicate rates (Holm-corrected $p < 10^{-14}$ for P1 vs P2 and P1 vs P3). Pre-registered expectation E1 (P1 majority duplicates) is supported; E2 (guarded paths $> 90\%$ reduction) is supported at multiplicity 5 but inconclusive at multiplicity 2 due to small sample size ($N = 30$).

However, moto is a mock environment, and the live pilot covers multiplicity 2 only. The research question for the registered factorial is fully answered only when the live AWS campaign (pilot-chosen $N = 1000$) is executed; \texttt{deliveries.jsonl} is currently empty.

\subsection{Objective 2: Measure Latency}

\textbf{Status:} Not met (moto unusable for cost/latency; live pilot has means at mult=2 only; full campaign empty).

Moto reports sub-5 ms mean latency for all paths, with no cold-start overhead. This is unrealistic: real AWS Lambda exhibits 100--500 ms cold-start latency and 1--10 ms warm-execution latency \citep{Scheuner2022CrossFit, Liu2023FaaSLight, Elhemali2022DynamoDB}. The latency measurements from moto are not cited as research findings.

Live AWS campaign data is required to quantify the latency overhead of P2 and P3 relative to P1, distinguishing cold starts from warm invocations, and reporting mean and p95 latency with 95\% confidence intervals.

\subsection{Objective 3: Measure Consumed Capacity}

\textbf{Status:} Not met (moto unusable for cost/latency; live pilot has means at mult=2 only; full campaign empty).

Moto reports fixed 0.5 WCUs for every DynamoDB write, which is incorrect. Real AWS DynamoDB bills 1.0 WCU for successful writes, 1.0 WCU for failed conditional checks, and 1.0 RCU for reads \citep{AWSDynamoDBDocs, Elhemali2022DynamoDB}. The capacity measurements from moto are not cited as research findings.

Live AWS campaign data is required to quantify the consumed capacity per request for P1, P2, and P3, including the cost of failed conditional checks (P2) and idempotency-record reads (P3).

\subsection{Objective 4: Correctness--Cost Surface and Comparison to Halfmoon}

\textbf{Status:} Partially met (correctness criterion validated; cost comparison pending live data).

The study confirms that P2 and P3 achieve the same correctness criterion as Halfmoon: \textit{no duplicate state mutation after retry}. The mechanism differs:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Halfmoon:} Custom runtime with asymmetric logging. Exactly-once by construction (formal proof). Throughput overhead 1.1--1.3$\times$ \citep{Qi2025Halfmoon}.
    \item \textbf{This study (P2, P3):} Application-level primitives on stock Lambda+DynamoDB. Empirically measured $\approx 0\%$ duplicates (not formally proven exactly-once). Latency and capacity overhead pending live AWS data.
\end{itemize}

The \textit{applicability trade-off} is clear: Halfmoon requires platform modification (unavailable on managed Lambda); P2 and P3 are immediately deployable but lack formal exactly-once guarantees and incur measurable latency/capacity costs (to be quantified by live AWS campaign).

\section{Practitioner Guidance}

Based on the moto functional validation (subject to confirmation by live AWS campaign data), the following guidance is offered to cloud application developers on managed FaaS platforms:

\subsection{When to Use Each Write Path}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Never use plain unconditional writes (P1) in production Lambda functions that may be retried.} P1 produces 100\% duplicate mutations, leading to data corruption, duplicate charges, or inconsistent state. The only acceptable use of P1 is for idempotent-by-nature operations (e.g., writing a deterministic hash to a cache, where overwriting with the same value is harmless).
    
    \item \textbf{Prefer conditional writes (P2) when the operation is naturally conditional.} Use P2 for operations like:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item "Write this item only if it does not already exist" (\texttt{attribute\_not\_exists(pk)}).
        \item "Update this item only if the version matches" (optimistic concurrency control).
        \item "Delete this item only if it still has the expected value".
    \end{itemize}
    P2 achieves 0\% duplicate mutations with minimal latency and capacity overhead (expected: sub-millisecond latency, zero extra capacity---failed conditional checks consume the same 1.0 WCU as successful writes).
    
    \item \textbf{Use idempotency-key patterns (P3) when the operation is non-idempotent or involves multi-step logic.} P3 generalises to any operation by caching the function's response and skipping replay on retry. Use P3 for operations like:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Sending an email or SMS (non-idempotent side effect).
        \item Charging a payment gateway (duplicate charges are unacceptable).
        \item Multi-step workflows where partial completion is possible.
    \end{itemize}
    P3 achieves 0\% duplicate mutations but incurs higher latency and capacity overhead (expected: 1--5 ms extra latency, 1--1.5 extra capacity units per retry for the idempotency-record read and write).
    
    \item \textbf{If you need formal exactly-once guarantees, consider custom runtimes or workflow orchestration.} Application-level primitives (P2, P3) achieve $\approx 0\%$ duplicates in practice, but do not provide the same formal guarantees as Halfmoon \citep{Qi2025Halfmoon} or workflow frameworks like AWS Step Functions \citep{Burckhardt2022Netherite, Zhuang2023ExoFlow}. For financial transactions, regulatory compliance, or mission-critical correctness, invest in a custom runtime or use a managed workflow orchestrator.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Implementation Best Practices}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{P2 implementation:} Always use \texttt{ReturnConsumedCapacity='TOTAL'} to monitor the cost of failed conditional checks. Catch \texttt{ConditionalCheckFailedException} and treat it as success (if the desired state is achieved). Log the failure for debugging and capacity analysis.
    
    \item \textbf{P3 implementation:} Use a deterministic request identifier (UUID from the event payload, not generated inside the function). Persist the idempotency record atomically with a conditional guard (\texttt{attribute\_not\_exists}) to handle races. Set a time-to-live (TTL) on idempotency records to avoid unbounded growth (e.g., 1 hour for transient retries, 24 hours for delayed retries).
    
    \item \textbf{Test for idempotence:} Use tools like Flux \citep{Ding2023Flux} to formally verify that your function is idempotent. Alternatively, write integration tests that invoke the function multiple times with the same input and assert that the external state (database, SQS queue, S3 bucket) reflects only one execution.
    
    \item \textbf{Monitor duplicate rates in production:} Instrument your Lambda functions to log whether each invocation is a first delivery or a retry (using CloudWatch Logs structured JSON). Aggregate logs to compute duplicate-mutation rates and alert on unexpectedly high rates.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodological Reflection}

\subsection{Strengths}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Controlled experiment with known retry counts:} The injected-timeout mechanism ensures that the number of retries is known before any mutations occur, eliminating ambiguity about what constitutes a "retry". This is superior to passively observing platform retries (where CloudWatch logs may under-report fast failures or double-count log streams).
    
    \item \textbf{Pilot-first replication:} Following \citet{Wen2025Variance}, the study uses a pilot to estimate variance and determine the full-campaign $N$, ensuring statistical power without over-sampling.
    
    \item \textbf{DynamoDB Streams as ground truth:} Using Streams to count mutations provides an independent, platform-level measurement that does not rely on the function's own logging. Cross-validation between Streams and self-reported logs (0 mismatches in moto validation) confirms data quality.
    
    \item \textbf{Reproducible artefact:} Pinned versions, deterministic seeding, and comprehensive tests make the artefact reproducible by other researchers.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations and Threats}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Moto validation only (live AWS pending):} The current results are from a mock environment. While moto correctly implements DynamoDB conditional-write semantics (allowing functional validation of P2 and P3), it does not model Lambda cold starts, network latency, or DynamoDB capacity billing. Live AWS data is required to answer the research question quantitatively.
    
    \item \textbf{Single region, single runtime:} Results may not generalise to other AWS regions (e.g., different network latencies, different Lambda instance types) or other runtimes (Node.js, Java, which have different cold-start characteristics).
    
    \item \textbf{Post-commit timeouts only:} The injected timeout occurs after the DynamoDB write has committed. Real-world failures (network partitions mid-transaction, partial writes, Lambda container crashes) may exhibit different behaviour. Future work should extend the methodology to inject faults at different stages of the request lifecycle.
    
    \item \textbf{On-demand capacity mode only:} Provisioned capacity mode (with reserved read/write capacity and throttling) may exhibit different latency and cost characteristics. Future work should evaluate P1, P2, and P3 under provisioned mode with various capacity settings.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What Would Be Done Differently?}

With hindsight, two design decisions could be revised:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Run the pilot on live AWS first, then moto validation.} The current workflow validated the implementation on moto before running the pilot on live AWS. While this reduces wasted AWS spend on buggy code, it delays the acquisition of real variance estimates (needed to determine $N$). A hybrid approach---moto for unit tests, then live AWS pilot immediately, then full campaign---would be more efficient.
    
    \item \textbf{Instrument cold vs warm latency from the start.} The current implementation distinguishes cold starts in post-hoc analysis (checking whether the invocation was the first in a sequence). A better approach would be to have the Lambda function report its own initialisation state (e.g., a global flag set after the first invocation) to avoid ambiguity.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work}

\subsection{Immediate Next Steps}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Run the live AWS pilot and campaign.} This is the most critical next step. The pilot will estimate variance, determine $N$, and validate that the expected duplicate rates (P1: 100\%, P2/P3: 0\%) hold on real AWS. The full campaign will provide statistically significant measurements of latency and capacity overhead.
    
    \item \textbf{Integrate live results into the report.} Replace the moto functional-validation results with live AWS results in Chapter 5 (Evaluation). Discuss any unexpected findings (e.g., if P2 or P3 exhibit non-zero duplicate rates, investigate root causes).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Extensions to the Experiment}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Multi-item transactions.} Extend the study to DynamoDB \texttt{TransactWriteItems}, which provide ACID guarantees across multiple items but consume 2 WCUs per item (vs 1 WCU for single-item writes) \citep{Idziorek2023DynamoDBTx}. Measure duplicate-mitigation effectiveness when a transaction spans business items and idempotency records.
    
    \item \textbf{Provisioned capacity mode.} Evaluate P1, P2, and P3 under provisioned capacity with various RCU/WCU allocations. Measure how throttling (when the application exceeds provisioned capacity) affects duplicate rates and latency.
    
    \item \textbf{Cross-region replication.} Investigate how P2 and P3 behave with DynamoDB global tables (eventual consistency across regions). Does a conditional write in region A immediately prevent a duplicate write in region B, or is there a replication lag window where duplicates can occur?
    
    \item \textbf{Alternative managed platforms.} Replicate the experiment on Google Cloud Functions + Firestore (which supports optimistic concurrency with version tokens) and Azure Functions + Cosmos DB (which supports conditional writes via ETag). Compare the duplicate-mitigation effectiveness and costs across the three major cloud providers.
    
    \item \textbf{Fault injection at different stages.} Extend the timeout injection to other failure points: (1) before the write (simulating a pre-commit timeout), (2) during the write (simulating a network partition), (3) after the write but before the response reaches the driver (simulating a proxy timeout). Measure whether P2 and P3 remain effective in all scenarios.
    
    \item \textbf{Production telemetry study.} Deploy a real-world Lambda application with P2 and P3, instrument it with detailed logging, and analyse duplicate rates, latency, and capacity over weeks or months. Compare the controlled-experiment results (where retries are injected deterministically) with production behaviour (where retries are triggered by real transient faults).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Broader Research Directions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cost-benefit framework for idempotency strategies.} Develop a decision framework that helps practitioners choose among P1 (no guard), P2 (conditional write), P3 (idempotency-key), and custom runtimes (Halfmoon) based on the application's correctness requirements, retry rate, and cost budget.
    
    \item \textbf{Formal verification of P2 and P3.} Use model checkers (TLA+, Alloy) or proof assistants (Coq, Isabelle) to prove that P2 and P3 are free of race conditions and achieve at-most-once mutation under specific assumptions (e.g., DynamoDB conditional writes are atomic, Lambda does not duplicate invocations within the idempotency-key TTL window).
    
    \item \textbf{Hybrid approaches.} Investigate combinations of application-level and runtime-level idempotency. For example, use P3 for the majority of requests (low overhead, good-enough guarantees) and fall back to a custom runtime (Halfmoon) for a small subset of high-value transactions (financial payments, inventory updates).
    
    \item \textbf{Serverless idempotency as a service.} Propose a managed service (analogous to AWS Powertools Idempotency utility, but platform-integrated) that automatically deduplicates Lambda invocations at the platform level, relieving developers of the burden of implementing P2 or P3. Analyse the feasibility, performance, and cost of such a service.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Final Remarks}

This research addresses a gap in the serverless computing literature: the empirical evaluation of application-level idempotency strategies on unmodified managed platforms. While custom runtime solutions (Halfmoon, Boki, CausalMesh) achieve exactly-once guarantees, they are unavailable to the majority of practitioners on AWS Lambda, Google Cloud Functions, or Azure Functions. The conditional writes (P2) and idempotency-key patterns (P3) documented by cloud providers are immediately applicable, but prior to this study, their duplicate-mitigation effectiveness and cost trade-offs were not quantified.

The moto functional validation confirms that P2 and P3 achieve 0\% duplicate mutations (vs 100\% for plain writes), providing practitioners with quantitative evidence to inform the choice of idempotency strategy. The pilot-first replication methodology, DynamoDB Streams-based ground truth, and reproducible artefact set a methodological standard for future serverless performance studies.

The live AWS campaign remains the critical next step. Once complete, the study will provide statistically significant measurements of latency and capacity overhead, enabling a full cost--benefit comparison of P1, P2, and P3, and contextualising the results against the Halfmoon baseline. The findings will inform practitioner decisions, guide cloud platform design, and advance the understanding of fault tolerance in serverless computing.
