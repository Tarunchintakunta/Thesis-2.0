Abstract
Federated Learning has grown as a powerful paradigm for developing intelligent cloud-native intrusion detection systems by allowing collaborative threat detection without sharing any type of sensitive network data across distributed cloud environments. Traditional system adopts a centralized architecture which gives privacy issues, overhead of the communication and scalability problems. To solve these problems this study proposes a Privacy-Preserving Federated Intrusion Detection Framework for Cloud-Native Environments, called SecureFL-IDS. The proposed approach combines Federated Learning with Differential Privacy by introducing the idea of sharing only lightweight differential privacy data. This allows suitable communication while maintaining differential privacy and increasing the scalability of the process of training a collaborative model. A quantitative experimental research methodology will be used and the framework will be built using TensorFlow Federated, Docker, Kubernetes, and Amazon Web Services (AWS). The framework will be tested using public benchmark intrusion detection datasets, under various cloud scenarios. The proposed solution will be evaluated based on intrusion detection metrics and cloud performance metrics like communication overhead, resource utilisation, convergence time and scalability. The expected result is a secure, scalable and intelligent cloud-based intrusion detection system that improves detection capabilities while minimizing communication overhead and allowing for good collaborative learning across cloud infrastructures.

Keywords: Cloud-Native Security, Federated Learning, Intrusion Detection System (IDS), Differential Privacy, Cloud Computing

Background of the Study
The concept of cloud-native computing becomes the foundation of modern digital services to deploy applications at scale (Veeravalli and Kiran, 2025). But, the distributed cloud-based environment presents unique cybersecurity challenges, and determining intrusion is more complex. However, traditional Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) mainly combines centralized architectures which can become limited and challenging to scale and secure data management (Sharma and Shambharkar, 2025). According to recent studies, Federated Learning (FL) shows a way to train a model collaboratively without sharing data from the network, which is a viable option for cloud security. On this basis, this study introduces a Federated Learning-based Privacy Preserving Intrusion Detection System (SecureFL-IDS) for Cloud-Native Environments to enhance the secure, scalable, and intelligent detection of intrusions in distributed cloud environments.
Research Problem Identification
Cloud native is the source of tremendous amounts of distributed traffic in the network constantly, which makes it harder to accurately and efficiently detect cyberattacks. While centralized IDSs are very effective in detecting intrusions, they also suffer from the problems of network data gathering at a central point, which raises privacy issues, communication overhead, and scalability concerns (Alenezi et al., 2026). Intrusion detection methodologies based on Federated Learning have recently been developed which limit data transfer, but still struggle with issues of communication efficiency, adaptation and real-time deployment in dynamically changing cloud environments. An intelligent cloud-centric solution like SecureFL-IDS will use collaborative intrusion detection, improve scalability and communication performance, better privacy security and overall security performance.
Research Question 
What is the effect of combining Federated Learning and Differential Privacy on the accuracy, privacy preservation and overall performance of cloud-native intrusion detection systems compared with traditional systems and existing federated learning-based approaches?
Potential Contributions and Benefits
The proposed research is expected to make an impact and contribution in the area of cloud computing by presenting SecureFL-IDS, a federated learning-based, cloud-native intrusion detection system that combines Federated Learning and Differential Privacy. The framework is anticipated to enhance the accuracy of intrusion detection and reduce communications overhead and expose of sensitive network information during the collaborative model training process. Moreover, the proposed model is designed to increase the scalability, enable safe collaboration between distributed cloud nodes, and optimize the working efficiency of cloud-based intrusion detection systems. The experimental evaluation will give hands-on evidence to build more secure, scalable and intelligent cybersecurity solutions for the modern cloud environments.

	•	Literature Review
This section mainly reviewed recent studies on cloud-native intrusion detection, communication-efficient federated learning, privacy-preserving learning and federated intrusion detection frameworks. The identified research gaps establish the need for the proposed SecureFL-IDS framework.
	•	Cloud-Native Intrusion Detection Systems

Cloud-native computing has revolutionized the way modern applications are deployed by using the power of cloud containers, microservices and orchestration platforms like Kubernetes for scalability, flexibility and better resource utilization (Boosa, 2025). These distributed environments raise the challenge of protecting cloud infrastructure from constantly changing cyber threats. The traditional intrusions detection systems (IDS) were designed for centralized architectures and are not quite good in detecting intrusions in dynamic cloud-native architectures where the workloads change continuously between different nodes. In recent years there have been research efforts to develop cloud-native IDS solutions by combining container monitoring, network traffic analysis and Kubernetes security features for improved threat detection and system performance (Naydenov et al., 2025). These methods provide greater visibility over the distributed cloud resources and can help detect attacks in real-time. Although these advances have been made, current solutions still have their limitations in the ability to support distributed attacks at scale, to withstand variations in workloads and to reduce any kind of communication overhead between cloud nodes. Hence it is important to develop intelligent and scalable systems which adapt well to modern cloud-native environment and maintain reliable security.

	•	Communication-Efficient Federated Learning in Cloud Environments
Recently, the performance of communication has been investigated in FL to reduce communication overhead while retaining the convergence and accuracy of the model in the cloud and edge scenario (Albshaier et al., 2025). The first study was introduced by (Luo et al., 2024) which proposed FedAda, an adaptive aggregation mechanism for client–edge–cloud federated learning, by dynamically determining the aggregation frequency by theoretical convergence analysis and node heterogeneity, which resulted in better performance in terms of accuracy and significantly reduced training time and communication overhead. Likewise, (Deng et al., 2024) introduced a hierarchical federated learning structure, where the optimal edge aggregator selection and node–edge association reduce communication cost and the diversity of data distribution at edge aggregators enhance the diversity of data use and learning accuracy without any unnecessary cloud aggregations. To meet the challenges of limited bandwidth, dynamic wireless environment, and data heterogeneity, the comprehensive survey in (Jia et al., 2025) summarized communication-efficient FL techniques, focused on communication compression, adaptive resource scheduling, and over-the-air computation (OTA) to compute data. The overall results of these studies suggest that adaptive aggregation, collaborative edges in a hierarchical fashion, and communication-aware optimization are all viable approaches to support scalable, efficient, and accurate federated learning in a heterogeneous cloud-edge setup.
	•	Privacy-Preserving Federated Learning for Cloud-Native Intrusion Detection
As the trend of cloud-native environments continues to grow, there is a greater demand to build intrusion detection frameworks with the capabilities of accurately detecting threats, maintaining data privacy and efficient collaborative learning across distributed cloud infrastructures. In this context, the two baseline studies both indicate that Federated Learning combined with Differential Privacy is a good solution for privacy-preserving intrusion and anomaly detection in cloud-native environments. Study (Saklani et al., 2026) introduces a Privacy-Preserving Cloud-Native Intrusion Detection System (PP-FL-DP-IDS) that combines the Federated Learning (FL) and Differential Privacy (DP) approaches to allow network traffic to be analyzed collaboratively across multiple cloud tenants while preserving privacy. 
The results of this study achieved the detection accuracy of 91.8% with F1 score above 90%, and low communication cost with stable federated training. Another study provided by (Li et al., 2026) (HierFedDP: A Hierarchical Privacy-preserving Federated Learning Framework for Anomaly Detection in Cloud Services) which proposed a hierarchical privacy-preserving federated learning framework for anomaly detection in cloud services. The architecture of the framework is a three-tier structure including edge servers, local clients and a central cloud server, with Differential Privacy being applied prior to the hierarchical model aggregation. This study improved the performance of anomaly detection to be similar to the conventional federated learning methods and decreased the communication overhead in WAN by 49%. While both frameworks effectively ensure the privacy of data, they have limited support for adaptive threat detection, intelligent communication optimisation, and the ability to thoroughly assess in dynamic cloud-native infrastructures. The limitations inspire the proposed SecureFL-IDS that seeks to boost collaborative IDS by efficient federated learning, lightweight differential privacy, and better deployment and scalability in the cloud.
	•	Federated Learning for Cloud-Native Intrusion Detection
Federated Learning (FL) has been recently shown to be a key technique for building privacy-preserving and scalable cloud-based intrusion detection systems (IDS). Study (Verma and Jailia, 2025) proposed a hybrid metaheuristic FL framework using the combination of Stacked Sparse Autoencoders and Fish School Search optimization to detect the attacks in multi-cloud networks and maintaining the data privacy, with an accuracy of 99.52%. Likewise, (Nellutla et al., 2025) presented a scalable distributed intelligence system based on FL, Kubernetes and streaming analytics, which achieved an improvement in the detection accuracy by 2.3% and communication overhead reduction of 34% compared to the traditional FL system. (Pr and Benitta, 2026) used an LSTM Autoencoder and CNN-GRU model to successfully identify intrusions and DDoS attacks with 96.3% accuracy using an AWS Free-Tier cloud-based FL system. Study (Laddi et al., 2024) proposed a federated machine learning-based intrusion detection model based on predictive analytics and unsupervised learning, where the data is not shared centrally, but the data is used to gain an adaptive cyberattack detection capability that respects privacy. 
Moreover, (P Selvaprasanth, 2025) proposed a federated learning-based IDS that integrated deep learning and feature optimization to not only attain a high accuracy of more than 95% for detecting various cyber threats, but also enhance the scalability and resilience. Last but not least (Mishra et al., 2026) combined reinforcement enhanced FL with LSTM, Random Forest, and Large Language Models to improve the intelligent intrusion detection and automatic threat reporting, with the accuracy of Random Forest being 99.77% and the federated global model obtaining around 90% accuracy. In summary, these studies demonstrate that FL can be used to significantly boost the detection performance of cloud-native intrusion detection system in terms of data privacy, communication cost, scalability and the high detection performance in distributed clouds.

	•	Research Gaps
While some existing research has showed the performance of Federated Learning, Differential Privacy, and cloud-native intrusion detection respectively, there are still some important gaps in the research. Most of the frameworks tackle intrusion detection accuracy, communication efficiency or privacy preservation but not all of them merge these capabilities into a single cloud-native solution. Moreover, there is little research assessing federated ID for dynamic cloud-native applications in a container orchestration platform like Kubernetes, and taking scalability, communication overhead and distributed deployment into account. The current methods offer also less support for efficient model aggregation and practical implementation in the cloud. To overcome these drawbacks, this research introduces SecureFL-IDS, a secure, intelligent and high-performance Federated Learning framework based on a lightweight Differential Privacy, communication-efficient model aggregation and scalable cloud deployment.

This section presented the proposed research methodology, implementation resources, performance evaluation framework, ethical considerations and project plan for the proposed SecureFL-IDS framework.
	•	Proposed Research Methodology
The methodology used for this research is quantitative experimental research that will be used to design, implement, and evaluate the proposed SecureFL-IDS (cloud-native intrusion detection) framework. The methodology centers on enhancing the accuracy of intrusion detection, communication efficiency and scalability in a distributed cloud environment, and ensuring that the required privacy is provided using lightweight differential privacy approaches as outlined in Table 2. The focus is to build an intelligent and efficient Cloud based intrusion detection system instead of delve into Privacy mechanisms. Research will start by gathering and pre-processing intrusion detection datasets publicly available that are suitable for federated learning. 
The datasets will be distributed among many simulated cloud clients, thus forming many simulated cloud tenants. A machine learning-based intrusion detection (ID) model will then be trained locally at each client, with no network data being transferred to a central server. Rather, only encrypted model parameters will be shared with a federated aggregation server to generate a global detection model. Lightweight differential privacy will be applied during model updating, to ensure further protection while reducing the amount of computation. The framework as proposed will be implemented in a containerized cloud-native environment with multiple clients communicating with the central aggregation server which will be implemented using Docker and Kubernetes. Lastly, controlled experiments will be performed under various cyber attacks to assess the framework's capabilities when compared to the traditional centralized intrusion detection systems and traditional federated learning with quantitative cloud security and system performance metrics.

	•	Research Resources
SecureFL-IDS will be done through the use of widely used cloud-native technologies, machine learning frameworks, and intrusion detection datasets that are publicly available. Python will be the main language for coding due to its extensive machine learning and data preprocessing (Marella and Palakurti, 2025) and cloud automation capabilities. The federated learning process will be implemented using TensorFlow Federated (TFF) and the model development, feature engineering, and data analysis will be done using TensorFlow, Scikit-learn, Pandas and NumPy. The experimental setup will be based on Docker and orchestrated by Kubernetes to emulate a real cloud-native environment with several distributed clients. 
The cloud infrastructure will be installed on AWS cloud as shown in Figure 1. The federated learning clients and aggregation server will be deployed on the Amazon EC2 instances, while Amazon S3 will securely store the datasets and trained models, and Amazon CloudWatch will continuously track the system performance, communication behaviour and resource usage for experimentation. Modern cloud network traffic and cyberattacks will be assumed to be represented by public benchmark datasets, like CICIDS2017, CSE-CIC-IDS2018, or TON_IoT. Prometheus and Grafana can be connected to further assess the deployment performance, providing detailed monitoring and visualization capabilities. The resources offer a scalable, practical, and reproducible cloud-based testbed for deployment and evaluation of the proposed SecureFL-IDS framework in various cloud workload and intrusion scenarios, while also enabling the benchmarking of the framework.

	•	Performance Evaluation Framework
For the proposed SecureFL-IDS, quantitative experiments are conducted to assess the effectiveness of the current framework in enhancing cloud-oriented IDS while keeping the distributed model training efficient. The experimental evaluation will be based on comparison of the proposed framework with the conventional centralized intrusion detection systems and the conventional federated learning without communication optimisation. Various attack scenarios, client engagement levels and cloud workload situations will be simulated to evaluate the framework in realistic operating environments. The accuracy, precision, recall, f1-score, False Positive Rate (FPR), and Area under the ROC Curve (AUC) will first be used to assess the ability of the model to correctly classify both normal and malicious network traffic. 
This research also aims at cloud-native deployment, so other cloud performance metrics such as training time, communication overhead, model convergence time, CPU utilisation, memory utilisation, network bandwidth consumption and system scalability will be measured (Silva et al., 2025). The trade-off between lightweight differential privacy and the overall detection performance will be examined, so that the security boosting also does not substantially reduce the model's effectiveness. The proposed SecureFL-IDS framework will be compared to baseline approaches and will be demonstrated whether intelligent federated intrusion detection can gain a better detection capability, a better scalability, a lower communication cost, and a high-efficient usage of cloud resources in today's cloud-native environments.
	•	Ethical and Responsible Research Considerations 
This proposed research does not involve human participants, interviews, surveys or the collection of personally identifiable information and therefore poses very little ethical risk. All experiments will be performed in the open benchmarks intrusion detection datasets which are specially developed for cybersecurity research. As these datasets are not private and sensitive personal information, privacy and ethical concerns are minimalized, and these datasets are extensively used in the research community. The proposed SecureFL-IDS is designed to be transparent in its design, implementation and evaluation, and thus the research will follow responsible scientific practices. All software products, machine learning libraries, cloud services and datasets will be utilized in compliance with the specific software licences and usage policies. All the experimental configurations, implementation procedures, hyperparameters and evaluation methods will be fully documented so that the results can be reproduced and be validated by future researchers. The lightweight differential privacy mechanism will be used to improve the secure collaborative model training process, but not the main research object. These ethical and responsible research practices will help to keep the study academically honest and provide reliable and repeatable type of knowledge in the cloud.
