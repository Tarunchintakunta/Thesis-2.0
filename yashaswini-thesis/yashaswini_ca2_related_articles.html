Thesis: Lightweight Fault Detection and Localisation in AWS Serverless Microservices: Accuracy and Monitoring Overhead against Deep-Learning Baselines

Research Question

What accuracy–overhead position does rule-based fault detection and localisation occupy relative to learned deep baselines in AWS serverless microservices?

This research introduces a Lightweight Graph-Guided Thresholding (LGGT) hybrid approach that fuses CloudWatch threshold-based detection with Isolation Forest anomaly scoring and X-Ray trace-dependency ranking. Detection and localisation accuracy are evaluated jointly with monitoring overhead (telemetry volume, latency, cost) — a pairing no prior study performs. The three articles below are the most directly related: the deep-learning accuracy ceiling being measured against, the benchmark substrate enabling like-for-like comparison, and the closest training-less detection precedent.

Article 1 — Accuracy Ceiling (Primary Baseline)

Article 1

Multi-Dimensional Anomaly Detection and Fault Localization in Microservice Architectures: A Dual-Channel Deep Learning Approach with Causal Inference for Intelligent Sensing

Xing, S., Wang, Y. and Liu, W.

Sensors, 25(11), 3396 (2025) — JCR Impact Factor 3.5, Q2

DOI: 10.3390/s25113396

Deep Learning TCN + VAE Causal Inference Fault Localisation

Summary

Xing et al. propose a dual-channel deep learning framework combining Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) with Variational Autoencoders (VAE), using contrastive learning for unified metric representations and causal inference for tracing fault propagation paths. Their semi-supervised evaluation achieves 95.4% detection accuracy, 93.8% F1-score, and 87.6% fault-component localisation precision, with a 43% reduction in average troubleshooting time and 31% reduction in false localisation rates versus existing state-of-the-art methods.

Relevance to Thesis

This paper establishes the primary accuracy ceiling against which the thesis positions its rule-based approach. Xing et al. represent the best published detection and localisation performance obtainable under ideal conditions — that is, with a labelled telemetry corpus and sufficient training compute. The thesis uses their F1 of 93.8% and localisation precision of 87.6% as the reference points for its pre-committed expectations: the rule-based arm should concede ≤10 percentage points of F1 and >10 points of top-3 localisation accuracy. Crucially, Xing et al. are cited as a ceiling reference, not a same-rig competitor, because the thesis explicitly acknowledges the asymmetry: the deep model's preconditions (large labelled corpus, training compute) are exactly what a newly deployed serverless service cannot supply.

Gap exposed: Accuracy is reported under ideal labelled-data conditions that a newly deployed, cost-constrained serverless service cannot meet. No monitoring overhead (telemetry volume, latency, cost) is reported. The model's preconditions — a large labelled failure corpus and training compute — are inapplicable to the target setting, not merely expensive.

Article 2 — Comparison Substrate (Benchmark)

Article 2

RCAEval: A Benchmark for Root Cause Analysis of Microservice Systems with Telemetry Data

Pham, L., Zhang, H., Ha, H., Salim, F. and Zhang, X.

Companion Proceedings of the ACM Web Conference 2025. New York: ACM, pp. 777–780 — CORE Ranking A*

DOI: 10.1145/3701716.3715290 · Code: github.com/phamquiluan/RCAEval

Benchmark Root Cause Analysis 735 Failure Cases 15 Baselines

Summary

Pham et al. publish an open-source benchmark comprising 735 labelled failure cases across three microservice systems (Online Boutique, Sock Shop, Train Ticket), with metrics, logs, and traces collected for each. The benchmark ships with 15 reproducible baselines covering metric-based, trace-based, and multi-source RCA approaches (including TraceRCA-style, BARO, and CIRCA-family methods), evaluated using AC@k and Avg@k metrics. The framework enables like-for-like comparison on identical data inputs.

Relevance to Thesis

RCAEval provides the mandatory comparison substrate for the thesis's second evaluation leg (like-for-like comparison). The thesis runs its rule-based LGGT detector and X-Ray localiser over the same 735 failure cases alongside the RCAEval baselines (BARO, CIRCA, TraceRCA, CausalRCA), ensuring that accuracy differences are measured on identical inputs rather than asserted across different datasets. This is what makes the comparison between an untrained rule-based method and trained deep baselines legible to one another. The thesis aligns its injected fault taxonomy to RCAEval's published categories to keep both evaluation legs commensurable.

Gap exposed: RCAEval supplies data and baselines but produces no findings of its own. It carries no cloud cost, latency, or telemetry-overhead dimension — exactly the overhead measurement the thesis adds in its third evaluation leg (AWS live artefact).

Article 3 — Training-Less Detection Precedent

Article 3

ReplicaWatcher: Training-Less Anomaly Detection in Containerized Microservices

El Khairi, A., Caselli, M., Peter, A. and Continella, A.

Proceedings of the 2024 Network and Distributed System Security Symposium (NDSS 2024) — CORE Ranking A*

DOI: 10.14722/ndss.2024.24286

Training-Free Anomaly Detection Replica-Based Containerized

Summary

El Khairi et al. dispense with training entirely, deriving a baseline at runtime from peer replicas of the same containerised service and flagging a snapshot whose dissimilarity from the replica set exceeds a calibrated threshold. The system achieves 91.08% average precision and 98.35% recall while remaining resilient against normality shifts — trained baselines degrade on software updates, but ReplicaWatcher maintains effectiveness without retraining because the definition of "normal" is drawn from live peers rather than historical data.

Relevance to Thesis

ReplicaWatcher is the closest methodological precedent for the thesis's core claim: that untrained detection need not concede substantial accuracy. El Khairi et al. provide direct evidence from a top-tier venue (NDSS, CORE A*) that training-free detection can match learned baselines (91% precision, 98% recall), which supports the thesis's pre-committed expectation that the rule-based arm stays within 10 pp of F1. However, the thesis goes further in three specific directions: (1) ReplicaWatcher presupposes several concurrent comparable replicas, which independently scaled serverless instances may not provide — the LGGT approach works from CloudWatch metrics of a single function; (2) ReplicaWatcher does not rank root causes (no localisation), while the thesis measures top-k and mean rank; (3) ReplicaWatcher does not price the observation, while the thesis reports overhead as a first-class evaluation dimension.

Gap exposed: Demonstrates training-free detection parity but provides no localisation ranking and no monitoring overhead measurement. Presupposes several concurrent comparable replicas — a precondition that independently scaled serverless functions may not satisfy.

How the Four Research-Niche Conditions Are Satisfied

The thesis niche requires all four conditions simultaneously; no prior study satisfies more than two:

  1. Evaluate an untrained detection/localisation method — the LGGT hybrid uses CloudWatch thresholds + Isolation Forest + X-Ray ranking, requiring no labelled history
  2. Compare against learned baselines on common data — via RCAEval (Pham et al., 2025) with BARO, CIRCA, TraceRCA, CausalRCA on identical 735 failure cases
  3. Measure localisation as well as detection — top-1, top-3 accuracy and mean rank of true root-cause service
  4. Report monitoring overhead — telemetry volume, added latency (tracing on vs off), estimated CloudWatch/X-Ray cost
