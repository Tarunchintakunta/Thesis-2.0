\begin{abstract}
Wanigasooriya \& Ekanayake (2026), ``NimbusGuard: A Novel Framework for Proactive Kubernetes Autoscaling Using Deep Q-Networks'' (IEEE ICOIN 2026), show that a DQN+LSTM proactive autoscaler beats reactive HPA and event-driven KEDA on responsiveness, but their own results table names the cost directly: NimbusGuard was ``the most agile and least stable system,'' with the highest average replica count and double the scaling events of the reactive baselines -- a trade-off they flag as future work rather than resolve. This project reproduces that trade-off on a synthetic cyclical workload with an unfiltered proactive scaler (\texttt{AggressivePAKS}: a feed-forward regressor forecasting next-step demand, acting immediately on every prediction), confirming it clearly beats reactive HPA on SLA violations (11.0 vs. 20.0 across 5 seeds) at the cost of more scaling events (384.4 vs. 407.4, comparable) and higher over-provisioning (45.8\% vs. 40.9\%). A stability-aware controller (\texttt{StabilityAwarePAKS}) is then built on the same predictor, adding exponential smoothing and a hysteresis/cooldown rule before acting -- the calmer design NimbusGuard names as future work but does not build. Across the same 5 seeds, it cuts scaling events by 57\% (165.8 vs. 384.4) and pod-count volatility by 32\% (1.95 vs. 2.86) relative to the aggressive baseline, while still beating reactive HPA on SLA violations (18.0 vs. 20.0) -- though by less than the unfiltered aggressive policy does (11.0). The result is an honest trade-off, not a clean win on every axis: stability is recovered substantially, at a partial cost to the SLA-violation benefit that made the proactive approach attractive in the first place.
\end{abstract}
