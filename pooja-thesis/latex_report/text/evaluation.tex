%% ===============================
\subsection{Experiment 1: Reproducing NimbusGuard's Trade-off}
\label{sec:evalOne}
%% ===============================

Aggressive PAKS reproduces the paper's central claim: proactive scaling roughly halves SLA violations relative to reactive HPA (11.0 vs. 20.0), at the cost of higher over-provisioning (45.8\% vs. 40.9\%). Scaling-event counts are comparable between the two here (384.4 vs. 407.4) rather than showing NimbusGuard's reported 2x gap -- a difference in the specific numbers that is expected, since this is a much simpler feed-forward predictor on a synthetic workload rather than a DQN+LSTM agent on a real Kubernetes testbed, but the qualitative trade-off (better SLA, worse over-provisioning) reproduces cleanly.

%% ===============================
\subsection{Experiment 2: Stability Recovered}
\label{sec:evalTwo}
%% ===============================

Stability-Aware PAKS is where the improvement shows up clearly: scaling events drop by 57\% (384.4 $\to$ 165.8) and pod-count volatility drops by 32\% (2.86 $\to$ 1.95) relative to Aggressive PAKS -- both well outside one standard deviation, and the specific instability NimbusGuard's own results table names as its weakness.

%% ===============================
\subsection{Experiment 3: The Honest Cost}
\label{sec:evalThree}
%% ===============================

The stability fix is not free. SLA violations for Stability-Aware PAKS (18.0) are higher than Aggressive PAKS (11.0) -- smoothing and cooldown necessarily mean reacting slightly later to genuine spikes, not just to noise. It still beats reactive HPA (20.0), but by a much smaller margin than the unfiltered aggressive policy does. Over-provisioning is essentially unchanged from Aggressive PAKS (46.5\% vs. 45.8\%) -- the stability fix targets scaling frequency and volatility specifically, not resource cost.

%% ===============================
\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%% ===============================

This is a genuine trade-off, not a strictly dominant improvement, and it is reported as such rather than only highlighting the metrics that favour the new design. The choice between Aggressive and Stability-Aware PAKS in a real deployment would depend on which is more expensive for a given operator: a handful of extra SLA violations, or a cluster that reconfigures itself 165 times instead of 384 over the same window. Section~\ref{sec:Conclusion} discusses how the smoothing/cooldown parameters could be tuned to move along this trade-off rather than treating the reported configuration as the only point on it.
