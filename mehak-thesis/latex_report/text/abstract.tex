\begin{abstract}
Cluster health monitoring traditionally relies on reactive threshold rules that only flag a problem after a metric has already crossed its limit. Recent work by Thapliyal (2026) proposes a proactive alternative for data-center SLA monitoring: a per-entity transformer with one attention head dedicated to each telemetry metric, predicting violations ahead of time. That paper's own results show a specific weakness -- the most volatile metric's head systematically underpredicts severity during high-load transients, because heads never exchange information with one another. This project reproduces that strict one-head-per-metric architecture as a baseline for cluster telemetry (CPU, memory, disk, network), confirms the same underprediction pattern on synthetic multivariate telemetry with injected cross-metric burst precursors, and evaluates a cross-head fusion layer that lets the per-metric heads attend to each other before classification. Across five training seeds, the fusion model gives a small, directionally consistent improvement on the targeted failure mode -- transient violation recall rises from 86.4\% to 88.4\% and mean underprediction bias falls from 0.29 to 0.27 severity levels -- at a marginal cost to overall accuracy (87.3\% to 86.3\%), with neither difference exceeding one standard deviation across seeds. Both attention-based models substantially outperform reactive threshold monitoring at anticipating violations before they occur (86-88\% vs. 2.5\% recall on future violations inside transient windows), confirming the baseline paper's central premise transfers to cluster telemetry, while the fusion improvement itself is real but modest.
\end{abstract}
